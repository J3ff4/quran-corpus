# Ayah of the Day — draft shortlist

> **Owner review.** Strike anything that should not appear. What survives becomes
> `AYAH_OF_THE_DAY` in `apps/mobile/src/home/ayahOfTheDay.ts` (M6b Task 5).
> Order does not matter — `ayahForDay` cycles the list by day number, so the
> rotation is deterministic and walks the whole list before repeating.

**118 candidates.** Every coordinate was validated against `surahs.ayah_count`
in the live corpus DB, and every one has an English translation row, so nothing
here can render blank. The gloss column is the stored English translation,
trimmed for this table only — the app shows it in full, in the user's chosen
content language.

At 118 entries the rotation repeats roughly every four months. Striking down to
60 makes it every two. Both are fine; below 30 the repeat becomes noticeable.

## Notes on the picks

- **Short surahs get one line, not all of them.** `112:1`, `113:1` and `114:1`
  are openings; `109:6` is deliberately the *closing* line, which is the one
  that stands alone. The card shows a single ayah, and four cards from
  al-Ikhlas in one week reads as a bug.
- **2:255** (Ayat al-Kursi) and **2:286** are in. **2:284** is not: it is
  commonly paired with 285-286 and reads oddly alone on a card.
- **33:56** is included; it is short and very widely known. Strike it if a
  standalone card feels wrong without its context.
- **Rulings in isolation are avoided** — no punishment or inheritance ayahs,
  because a card with no context is the worst possible frame for them. One
  exception is in the list on purpose: **2:216**, which opens on fighting and
  is remembered for its second half ("perhaps you hate a thing and it is good
  for you"). It is the only one of its class here. Strike it if the opening
  clause on a home card is not what you want.
- If you want anything from the classes above added, name it — I have not
  guessed at them.
